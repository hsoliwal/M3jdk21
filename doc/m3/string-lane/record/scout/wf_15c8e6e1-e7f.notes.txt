Evaluated and not proposed.
- Slices of one admitted chunk for stream-backed BufferedReader: the per-character pass count stays the same (snapshot, scan, hash, copy move from per line to per chunk); lines stop being canonical atoms; each retained line pins up to 8192 units of native memory against the pool budget, whose exhaustion runs System.gc() and sleeps in M3StringPool.reserveSlow (803-835); hashCode of a range computes full range facts. Only the StringReader case (RD-3), where the buffer is already admitted, is a clear gain.
- A trusted no-snapshot char ingress for java.io would need a new JavaLangAccess method (a public member of an internal interface); RD-2 removes the snapshot inside java.lang for every caller without one.
- Folding all facts during decode: not free (see RD-6).

Cross-angle observations seen while tracing (for the operations and precompute lanes, not candidates here).
- trim / strip on a freshly read line pay the whole facts fold: String.trim 4287-4290 -> M3StringPrecomputeBridge.trim 147-151 calls source.facts() only to read trimStart / trimEnd; stock scans the two edges.
- hashCode of any slice (substring, lines() result) is facts().javaHash for a range (M3String.java 690-692), a full fold instead of one multiply-add pass.
- Admission hashes every unit with a full splitmix finalizer (M3StringPool.mix64 843-850, two multiplies per unit, serial); this likely dominates admission cost. Changing it may touch the mapped lexicon image (not verified).
